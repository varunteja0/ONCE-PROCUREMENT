What does this mean?

Once issues a receipt every time a supplier submission is delivered to a buyer portal. Each receipt contains the immutable facts of that submission (who submitted what, where, and when) and is signed with an Ed25519 private key held by Once. The signature covers the receipt's canonical-JSON encoding (RFC 8785), so even a single byte changed anywhere in the payload would invalidate it.

A verified result means two things: (1) the receipt was signed by a key Once controls, and (2) the payload you are looking at is byte-for-byte the payload that was signed. That gives you cryptographic certainty about the contents of the submission record.

Verification does not by itself guarantee legal admissibility, the correctness of the underlying business process, or that the supplier portal actually accepted the data. It is evidence of integrity, not of business outcomes. Pair it with your own audit trail when stakes are high.
